> **An old app, revived.** This app was first made around 2019 and was on Google Play until the account was closed. In 2026 it was rebuilt for modern Android phones with a new design, mainly to show it again. Please don't expect it to be actively maintained.

**The Great Postman is a free REST API client for Android, like Postman on your phone. Send any HTTP request and read the response as clean JSON, wherever you are.**

## The problem

When you build or use an API, you often need to send a quick request and see what comes back. Usually that means sitting at your computer with Postman or a terminal. Away from your desk, there is no easy way to test an API.

## How The Great Postman solves it

Build any request on your phone, send it, and read the answer in a clean view. If you use Postman on your computer, you will feel at home.

1. **Send any request:** GET, POST, PUT, PATCH, DELETE, HEAD and OPTIONS, over HTTP or HTTPS.
2. **Full control:** query params, path params, headers, body (JSON, text, form or multipart) and auth (Bearer or Basic).
3. **Easy-to-read responses:** a pretty, collapsible JSON tree, raw text or headers, with status, time and size. Copy, share or search it.
4. **History** of every request, searchable.
5. **Collections** to save requests, and **environments** for values like {{baseUrl}}.
6. **Import and export cURL.**
7. **Free, no ads,** with light and dark mode.

## Who it is for

1. Backend and mobile developers testing their APIs on the go.
2. Testers who want to check an endpoint quickly from a phone.
3. Students learning how REST APIs work.

## The story

This was a group project, built with friends. I had a Google Play account, and we published whatever we could build together. Most of my friends hadn't studied computer science; some came from electrical and telecommunication engineering. I was teaching them how to make apps, so we kept each app as simple as possible and as nice as we could, so people would want to download it.

We don't work together any more, but I'm proud of what came out of it. Somehow I made them curious about programming, and after just a few days of building they loved it. Alhamdulillah, many of them still work in software development today, even though they didn't come from that background.
